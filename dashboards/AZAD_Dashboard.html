Investment Thesis

Azad Engineering is one of four companies globally that can manufacture critical rotating components for gas, steam, and nuclear turbines — a qualification moat that took 15–17 years to build and cannot be replicated in less than a decade. With ₹6,500 crore order book (11x FY26 revenue), 4 dedicated OEM facilities commissioned in FY25–26, and wallet share below 1.5% of customers' relevant spend, the platform for a multi-year compounding story is firmly in place.

The investment case at current prices rests on two time-boxed catalysts: (1) operating leverage unlock in FY27 as newly-commissioned Phase 1 capacity reaches committed throughput — EBITDA margin could surprise above 38–39% as fixed cost absorption improves; and (2) the Rolls-Royce/P&W/Safran qualification pipeline converting to serial revenue in FY27–28, shifting aerospace mix from 17% toward 25–30% and re-rating the earnings quality.

At ₹2,046 (100x FY26 EPS), the stock is not cheap — it prices in 25%+ growth through FY28. The margin of safety is thin, but the business quality is exceptional and the TAM is genuinely USD 28–100 billion with Azad at sub-1% wallet share. ACCUMULATE on dips, full position below ₹1,850.

Business Description

Azad Engineering manufactures precision-critical rotating components for the world's largest OEMs in Energy, Aerospace & Defence, and Oil & Gas. Its products — 3D airfoils, turbine blades, nozzle vanes, compressor blades, impellers, and engine rotating assemblies — are life-critical and project-critical, meaning a single failure in service can result in catastrophic outcomes. This creates an extreme quality and qualification barrier that takes 3–5 years per product to certify, and 15–17 years to build a meaningful qualification library.

The company operates a "factory-within-factory" model — 4 dedicated lean manufacturing facilities, each built exclusively for one OEM (MHI, GE Vernova, Siemens Energy, Baker Hughes). Each facility mirrors the OEM's own production standards, allowing seamless integration into their global supply chain. This model deepens stickiness, reduces qualification risk on new products, and sets the baseline for 8-year single-source agreements.

30–48 month OEM qualification per product. Zero-PPM requirement means OEM never voluntarily re-opens a qualified supplier slot. Average customer relationship 10+ years. Multi-program engagements deepen lock-in.
Regulatory / Approvals STRONG
AS9100, NADCAP (special processes), EDF/Arabelle nuclear certification (only Indian company), NABL-accredited testing lab, multiple OEM-specific quality certifications. Nuclear qualification alone took years.
Scale & Intangibles STRONG
1,700+ product qualifications accumulated over 15+ years. 45+ special process approvals. Replicating this library from scratch takes 15–17 years (energy) or 5 years (aerospace). The IP is the qualification library.
Network Effects MODERATE
OEM relationships are sticky — once Azad is in the bill of materials for one component, new component wins at the same OEM are faster (shared quality records, process documentation). Cross-OEM qualification network accelerating.
Cost Advantage MODERATE
India wage advantage vs Western peers. Domestic raw material qualification (Sunflag, Star Wire for Nimonic/Inconel) reducing import dependency. Backward integration (coatings, heat treatment in-house) saves 2–3% EBITDA.
Brand STRONG (B2B)
MHI Global Partner of the Year 2024. GE Vernova Awards for Operational Excellence. Honeywell, Godrej Aerospace recognitions. "Azad" is a known quality brand at every major gas turbine OEM globally.

Structural Tailwinds
  • ⚡
    Global energy transition driving gas turbine supercycle. 47% of turbines under construction (82 GW) are hydrogen-ready — Azad's customers (GE, MHI, Siemens) are at the centre. GE Vernova alone has 55 GW under construction. Gas turbines are the bridge fuel and peaker solution for the AI data centre buildout — demand for compute is growing 3x/year, gas is the only scalable near-term power source.
  • ✈️
    Commercial aviation supercycle. 21,000+ aircraft due for replacement 2024–44. Global commercial fleet to grow 32% to 38,300 aircraft by 2035. Boeing + Airbus backlog 15,000+ narrow-bodies. P&W GTF engine issues creating demand for precision parts. India's fleet alone: 890 aircraft with 2,325 on order.
  • 🛡️
    India defence indigenisation programme. Defence production to triple from ₹94,845 Cr (FY22) to ₹3 lakh Cr (FY29E). First indigenous jet engine (ATGG/GTRE) being built by Azad — landmark programme. Exports targeted at ₹50,000 Cr by FY29. HAL, DRDO procurement shifting to domestic suppliers.
  • ☢️
    Nuclear power renaissance. India targets 22,000 MW nuclear capacity by 2050 (from current ~7,000 MW). Global nuclear generation growing 2%/year. Azad is only Indian company EDF/Arabelle-certified for nuclear turbine components — first mover in a market that just opened.
  • 🏭
    Steam turbine services market expanding. Global steam turbine service market: USD 19.5 bn (2024) → USD 31.8 bn (2034, CAGR 5%). Installed base maintenance drives steady replacement demand for turbine blades — Azad's core competency.
Key Risks to Industry Thesis
  • ⚠️
    Energy transition to renewables could reduce gas turbine orders. If solar + storage costs fall faster than expected, gas turbine order books could disappoint from FY28+. However, Azad's nuclear and steam turbine exposure partially offsets this.
  • ⚠️
    Geopolitical risk on exports (93% revenue). Trade restrictions, sanctions, or OEM supply chain regionalization (US, Europe sourcing more locally) could slow Azad's global growth. Chinese competitors (AECC, Wuxi) may benefit from domestic preference in China-linked supply chains.
  • ⚠️
    OEM consolidation or programme cancellation. GE Vernova, Siemens Energy, or MHI pivoting away from existing turbine platforms (e.g., hydrogen turbines may require new part qualifications from scratch) could partially obsolete current qualification library.
