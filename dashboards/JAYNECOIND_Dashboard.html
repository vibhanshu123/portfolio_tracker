Business Description

Jayaswal Neco is a fully integrated steel producer headquartered in Raipur, Chhattisgarh, with roots dating to 1972. Its core Siltara plant is a blast-furnace-to-rolling-mill value chain: captive iron ore mines (Chhotedongar + Metabodeli) feed a pellet plant, which feeds the BF, which feeds SMS and rolling mills producing TMT bars, wire rods, structurals, and billets. The company also operates foundry castings plants in Nagpur and Anjora (automotive grey iron castings). Captive power from a 4 MW solar + 3.5 MW windmill unit reduces grid dependency.

Structural Tailwinds (5)
🏗️ Infrastructure Push
Government capex ₹11.1 lakh Cr budget FY26. PMGSY, NHAI, railways, housing — all TMT-intensive. Neco's CG location near infra spending centre.
🏠 Affordable Housing (PMAY-U 2.0)
3 Cr urban housing units under PMAY-U 2.0. Each unit ~1.5 MT TMT = 4.5 Cr MT additional TMT demand over 5 years. Neco's bread-and-butter product.
⚡ Captive Ore Scarcity Premium
Auction-based iron ore increasingly expensive for non-captive players. Neco's Chhotedongar + Metabodeli leases = structural cost advantage of ~₹1,500–2,000/MT vs market.
📦 China +1 Engineering Exports
Global OEMs seeking India sourcing for castings (Neco Castings segment). Low-value but steady margin contributor with potential for growth as supply chains diversify.
🔋 Scrap Shortage Drives BF Value
India's scrap availability insufficient for induction/EAF route to dominate. BF-BOF route remains essential for quality long products. Neco's BF capacity stays relevant for 10+ years.
Structural Headwinds
🇨🇳 Chinese Overcapacity / Dumping Risk
China's 1 Bn MT+ capacity exports excess steel when domestic demand slows. HRC/coil prices are globally set by Chinese marginal cost. While BIS norms protect TMT, indirect pressure via scrap/billets pricing remains.
⛏️ Coking Coal Import Dependency
India imports ~85% of coking coal requirements. Neco's BF route is fully dependent on imported coking coal (Australia/Russia/USA). A 30% coal price spike = ~₹500/MT cost increase, ~₹350 Cr EBITDA drag at current volumes.

India imports ~85% of coking coal; Neco's BF route is fully dependent. FY20 coking coal spikes pushed costs sharply. A 30% price increase adds ~₹350–400 Cr to COGS annually at current BF volumes. Company has no hedging or domestic coal supply contract.
Quantified: 30% coal spike → EBITDA margin compressed 4–5 ppts → ~₹300–350 Cr EBITDA reduction
Mitigant: Russian coal availability has improved since FY23; Australia coal prices softening in current cycle.
Blast Furnace Operational Risk
MEDIUM
Q1FY25 BF shutdown demonstrated single-event risk: BF production fell from 5.72 lakh MT to an equivalent ~1.5 lakh MT in Q1FY25, dragging H1FY25 EBITDA to ₹341 Cr (vs ₹650 Cr H1FY26). BF relining/repairs are capital-intensive and can last 60–90 days.
Quantified: A Q1FY25-style BF shutdown in FY27 → FY27 EBITDA reduced by ~₹200–300 Cr
Mitigant: BF just completed reline in FY25 — next major maintenance likely 4–5 years away at normal campaign life.
